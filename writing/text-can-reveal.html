What can our words reveal about us?

Privacy questions do not end when names and contact details disappear from a dataset.

Apostolis Siatras  ·  20 September 2026

Text contains clues. The topics we discuss and the language we use can support inferences about us, even when a system does not receive an explicit personal identifier.

Unmasking Users in IoC, coauthored with Asimina Tsouplaki, George Mikros, and Christos Kalloniatis, examines this problem through language-model profiling. The paper compares GPT-4o and Llama 3-70B using blog and Reddit data.

Look at what a system can infer

The useful design question is broader than “Which identifying fields have we removed?” It also asks which sensitive attributes the remaining information allows a model to infer, and what happens when those inferences are wrong.

The experiments use online text datasets. Their implications for connected cloud systems should be read in that context, rather than as a deployment study of a live infrastructure.

Read the research

Tsouplaki, A., Siatras, A., Mikros, G., & Kalloniatis, C. (2026). Unmasking Users in IoC: LLMs, Text Profiling, and Privacy Implications. International Journal of Information Security, 25, 92.
